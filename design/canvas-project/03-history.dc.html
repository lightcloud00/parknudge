History, and the display limit

The one screen where the free boundary is visible, so it is also the screen most likely to be read as a hostage note. The design answers that by showing the older sessions still sitting on the device.

HueH 205

1

A display limit, not a retention limit

The hidden sessions are drawn as real dimmed rows, not an empty upsell. They exist on the device and the copy under the list says so in as many words.

Docs/PRODUCT.md — “A display limit is not a retention limit.”
2

Three, and the count is stated

“3 of 12 shown” sits above the list rather than being discovered at the bottom. The user learns the boundary before they hit it.

Free tier — three newest completed sessions.
3

Nothing is ever deleted

A missing, revoked or refunded entitlement hides locked surfaces and nothing else — sessions, costs, photos and reminder metadata all survive.

Docs/MONETIZATION.md — Data preservation.
4

Export is locked, and shows it in the bar

CSV export is Pro, so the toolbar carries a lock rather than a share glyph that fails on tap. A control that cannot act should not look like it can.

HistoryView.swift — export toolbar item.
5

The upsell is a row, never a wall

It sits under the content it refers to, states the real price, and cannot cover a session the user is trying to read. Tapping the locked row reaches the same screen.

Docs/MONETIZATION.md — reachable from a locked feature.
6

Export warns before it writes

The confirmation names what the file contains — dates, coordinates, labels, notes, meter times and costs — because location history is the sensitive part.

HistoryView.swift — “Export sensitive location history?”
